Laboratory biomarkers

A short guide to some of the markers discussed in consultations.

Lipid profile

Measures cholesterol fractions and triglycerides in the blood.

ApoB

A protein carried on atherogenic lipoprotein particles such as LDL; it reflects the number of these particles.

Lp(a)

A genetically determined lipoprotein whose level is largely stable through life.

hs-CRP

A sensitive marker of low-grade inflammation.

HbA1c

Reflects average blood glucose over roughly the previous two to three months.

Insulin

A hormone that regulates blood glucose; together with fasting glucose it can be used to estimate insulin resistance (HOMA-IR).

Offered services

Dr Daka offers five types of consultation, online on Google Meet or in person in Tirana, at a time chosen beforehand. Each consultation helps you understand your laboratory results, assess your cardiometabolic risk and agree a prevention and monitoring plan.

Consultation types

30–40 minutes

Laboratory Results Interpretation

Interpretation of biochemical and metabolic laboratory results, including glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile, liver and kidney markers, thyroid parameters and inflammatory biomarkers.

40–45 minutes

Cardiovascular Risk Assessment

Assessment of lipid profile, ApoB, ApoA1, Lp(a), hs-CRP, homocysteine, glucose metabolism and relevant clinical risk factors.

40–45 minutes

Insulin Resistance and Metabolic Health

Assessment of fasting glucose, insulin, HOMA-IR where appropriate, HbA1c, lipid abnormalities, metabolic syndrome and associated risk factors.

45–60 minutes

Preventive Metabolic Health Consultation

Comprehensive review of metabolic risk, inflammation, lifestyle factors, laboratory findings and an individualised prevention and monitoring plan.

20–30 minutes

Follow-up Consultation

Review of progress and new results.

When a consultation helps

  • Understanding biochemical and metabolic results: glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile, liver, kidney and thyroid markers
  • Cardiovascular risk assessment: lipids, ApoB, ApoA1, Lp(a), hs-CRP and homocysteine
  • Insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome
  • An individualised prevention and monitoring plan
  • Reviewing progress and new results over time

When it is not suitable

  • Urgent cases or acute symptoms: contact emergency services
  • Physical examination, which needs an in-person visit
  • Starting or changing treatment: this remains with your treating doctor
  • Interpretation without the relevant results or documentation
  • Online consultations for patients located outside Albania, for now

A consultation does not replace a physical examination and is not suitable for urgent cases. If you have acute symptoms, contact emergency services immediately.

Frequently asked questions

How long does booking take?

About three minutes: choose a type of visit and consultation, pick a free slot and enter your details.

What happens after I send my request?

You receive an email confirming the date and time. For online consultations it includes the Google Meet link, and it explains how to share your laboratory results securely.

Can I choose an in-person visit?

Yes. Select “In person” under type of visit when you book. The same consultation types and fee apply.

What do I receive afterwards?

A written consultation summary with the relevant findings, recommendations, further investigations if indicated and a follow-up plan.

Can a consultation replace my own doctor?

No. It supports your understanding of laboratory results and risk. Decisions on diagnosis and treatment remain with your treating doctor.
